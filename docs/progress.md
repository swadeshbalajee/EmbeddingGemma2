## Data-length finding

The current 200K train cache has median decoder target lengths of 11 tokens for image captions, 14 for audio, 19 for video, and 163 for prose. 82.9% of image captions are 16 tokens or fewer. This supports adding detailed but still image-like descriptions. Long general prose alone would shift the output style away from captioning.

## Literature and working hypothesis

- [ClipCap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09734) established a learned visual-embedding-to-language-model prefix mapping, including multi-token prefixes.
- [CapDec](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-emnlp.299/) showed that a text-trained embedding decoder can fail on images because of a modality gap, and studied noise injected into text embeddings.
- [DeCap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03032) studied a training-free projection of visual embeddings toward a text-embedding support set at inference.
- [DOCCI](https://research.google/pubs/docci-descriptions-of-connected-and-contrasting-images/) documents why common image captions omit detail. [ShareGPT4V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12793) provides a large caption source to investigate for longer text-only targets.

The first comparison will prioritize changes that can train quickly on the existing pooled 768-d cache: the completed one-token V1-B baseline, a noise-regularized one-token decoder, and an eight-token prefix decoder. Feature-wise 768-token cross-attention and full hidden-state cross-attention require substantially more compute or a different cache and are poor fits for this short experiment. All reported variants will use the same evaluation items and decoding settings.

The eight-token model is our practical hybrid of noise-regularized text-only training and learned multi-token prefixing. It cannot recover information absent from the pooled 768-d embedding, but it gives the small decoder several learned views of that embedding at every causal-attention layer. The one-token noise model isolates the proposed modality-gap regularizer; comparing it with the eight-token noise model isolates prefix capacity. This is a fast architectural test of whether greater conditioning bandwidth improves transfer before attempting expensive token-level encoder–decoder cross-attention.

Interpret the eight-versus-one comparison as a change in learned prefix design, not a pure capacity ablation: eight tokens also shift text positions and change attention competition. A repeated-token or parameter-matched control would be appropriate in a later study; it is outside this quick three-run poster comparison.
